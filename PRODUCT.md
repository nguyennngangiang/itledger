# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

IT Ledger is used inside one company by two overlapping audiences. The IT / operations team are the admins of record: they enter and maintain the data — registering devices, logging repairs, recording handovers, and keeping owners current. Team leads and managers are the readers: they browse the fleet to see what their people hold and check on device status, without owning data entry. The IT team works in the tool daily and needs speed; managers drop in occasionally and need to understand what they're looking at without training.

## Product Purpose

IT Ledger is the company's system of record for IT hardware — every device, its current owner, and its full maintenance and handover history — with a dashboard that rolls it all up into fleet stats. It exists to replace scattered spreadsheets with one trustworthy source. Success is measured by fleet visibility and reporting: leadership and managers can see fleet health, hardware mix, aging devices, and lifecycle status at a glance, and trust that the numbers are current because the tool is pleasant enough that the IT team actually keeps it up to date.

## Positioning

IT Ledger makes a tedious internal chore feel fast and even enjoyable to keep current — an asset tracker people don't dread opening. That pleasantness is the strategic wedge: a ledger only reports accurately if it's kept current, and it only stays current if using it isn't a burden.

## Brand Personality

Playful and delightful leads, resting on a foundation of soft enterprise polish. The visual base is calm and trustworthy — pastel indigo, Inter, gentle depth — but the character comes through in motion and small moments: click sparkles, springy feedback, an interface that feels alive rather than clinical. The tone is warm and light, never corporate or joyless. Delight is the point, not decoration, because the product's whole reason to exist is making people want to keep the ledger current. The line to walk: personality that makes the tool memorable without ever getting in the way of the task.

## Anti-references

Not a dense legacy admin panel — no cluttered gray enterprise software (the old SAP / ServiceNow feel) that's intimidating and joyless. Not a raw spreadsheet — never just a grid of cells with no hierarchy, no context, no sense of the story behind a device. Not generic AI-dashboard slop — avoid purple-gradient hero-metric cards, identical icon+title+text card grids, and decorative fluff that signals "template."

## Design Principles

Delight in a chore. The tool should make a tedious task feel fast and pleasant; every friction removed and every moment of warmth added serves the goal of keeping the ledger current.

Story over cells. A device is never just a current-state row — it carries its owner, maintenance, and handover history. Surface that context so the interface reads as a record with a narrative, not a spreadsheet.

Visibility at a glance. Managers should understand fleet health without digging or training. The rollups and status cues do the explaining.

Familiar affordances, with personality. Use standard admin patterns (top nav, tables, modals sparingly) so nothing confuses a first-time manager — then add character in the seams where it costs no clarity.

Motion that always has a fallback. Playful motion is core to the identity, not optional garnish; but every sparkle and spring ships with a reduced-motion alternative and never blocks content from rendering.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: body text at ≥4.5:1 contrast (large text ≥3:1), full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, and honored `prefers-reduced-motion`. Because playful motion is central to the brand, reduced-motion fallbacks get first-class attention — crossfades or instant transitions in place of sparkle bursts and springs — so the tool stays fully usable and comfortable for motion-sensitive users.
